# What to do

Stay aware by tracking where your sales go, learning the thresholds for the states you sell into most, and getting professional help before obligations build up. The goal is to catch nexus early rather than discover it after the fact.

## The awareness checklist

- Keep a rough running total of sales by state, so you can see thresholds approaching.
- Know whether each sales channel is a marketplace that collects for you or a direct channel where you are responsible.
- Note where your inventory is physically stored, including marketplace fulfillment centers.
- Note any employees, contractors, or physical ties by state.
- When sales into a state grow, check that state's current economic nexus threshold.

## When to get help

Bring in a qualified sales tax professional when sales are growing across many states, when you are approaching or have crossed thresholds, when you sell through both marketplaces and a direct channel, or simply when you are unsure. The cost of a consultation is small compared with the cost of unaddressed back taxes and penalties. Getting help early is cheaper than fixing a problem later.

## Why not to guess

Sales tax rules are state-specific, detailed, and changing, which makes guessing risky. A reasonable-sounding assumption can be wrong for a particular state, and the consequences fall on the seller. This reference is meant to make you aware of what to watch for, not to substitute for advice on your actual situation. For anything that carries real obligation, confirm with a professional.

## The honest bottom line

Nexus is one of those obligations that is easy to ignore until it is a problem. The whole value of awareness is acting before that point: tracking your sales footprint, knowing roughly where you stand, and getting help at the right time. None of this is a filing plan, and none of it is advice for your specific case. It is the early-warning system that keeps sales tax from becoming a surprise.
