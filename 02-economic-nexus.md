# Economic nexus

Economic nexus means a state can require you to collect its sales tax based solely on how much you sell into that state, with no physical presence needed. Each state sets its own threshold, commonly a level of sales revenue or a number of transactions in a year.

## What changed

A landmark court decision opened the door for states to base nexus on economic activity rather than physical presence. In the years since, states broadly adopted economic nexus rules. The practical effect is that crossing a state's sales threshold can create an obligation to collect that state's sales tax, even for a seller located entirely elsewhere. Physical presence is no longer required for an obligation to attach.

## How thresholds work

Each state sets its own economic nexus threshold, and the specifics differ. Thresholds are commonly defined by a dollar amount of sales into the state over a period, sometimes combined with or replaced by a count of separate transactions. Because the numbers and the rules vary by state, the same total sales could cross the threshold in one state and not another. There is no single national number to watch.

## Why this is easy to miss

A seller can cross thresholds gradually and unknowingly, because online sales scatter across many states without any obvious signal. There is no notice when you cross a line; the obligation simply exists once the threshold is met. By the time a seller realizes nexus applies, sales may have been happening untaxed for some time, which is how back-tax exposure builds up quietly.

## The takeaway

The core awareness point is that volume alone can create tax obligations in states where you have no other connection. As sales grow and spread, the number of states where you might have economic nexus grows with them. Tracking where your sales go, and roughly how much goes to each state, is the practical habit that keeps this from becoming a surprise.
