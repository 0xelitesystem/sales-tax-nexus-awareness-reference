# What nexus is

Sales tax nexus is a connection between a seller and a state that is strong enough to obligate the seller to collect and remit that state's sales tax. Traditionally nexus came from physical presence; now it can also come from economic activity alone.

## The two kinds

There are two broad types of nexus. Physical nexus comes from a tangible presence in a state, such as an office, employees, inventory stored there, or other physical ties. Economic nexus comes purely from the amount of business done with customers in a state, measured by sales revenue or transaction count, with no physical presence required. Either type can create an obligation to collect that state's tax.

## Why it exists

Sales tax is administered at the state level, and each state taxes sales that have a sufficient connection to it. Nexus is the legal test for whether that connection is strong enough to require a seller to act as a tax collector for the state. Without nexus, a seller generally has no obligation to collect a given state's sales tax; with nexus, the obligation attaches.

## Why it matters to small sellers

For a long time, a seller with no physical footprint in a state could sell there without collecting its sales tax. Economic nexus changed that, so a purely online seller can now have obligations in many states based on sales volume alone. Small sellers are affected because the thresholds, while set to exclude the very smallest activity, can be crossed faster than expected once a product sells across the country.

## The shape of the obligation

Where nexus exists, the seller is generally responsible for registering with the state, collecting the correct sales tax from buyers in that state, and remitting it on a schedule. The tax is the buyer's, but the seller is the collector and bears the responsibility for doing it right. Understanding nexus is therefore the first step in knowing where these collector duties might apply to you.
