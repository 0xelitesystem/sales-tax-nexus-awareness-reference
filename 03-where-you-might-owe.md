# Where you might owe

You might have a sales tax obligation in any state where you have physical presence or where your sales cross that state's economic nexus threshold. As sales spread across the country, the number of states where obligations could apply grows.

## Obligations stack by state

Each state is its own jurisdiction with its own rules, thresholds, rates, and filing requirements. Nexus in five states means five separate sets of obligations, not one. This is what makes sales tax compliance more involved than a single tax: a growing online business can find itself potentially responsible to many states at once, each with its own threshold and process.

## Physical ties you might overlook

Physical nexus can come from connections that are easy to forget. Storing inventory in a state, including in a fulfillment center operated by a marketplace, can create physical nexus there. Employees or contractors located in a state, or even attending certain events there, may count. The point is that physical nexus is broader than just having an office, and the less obvious ties are the ones sellers miss.

## The rate complication

Beyond knowing where you owe, sales tax rates vary not just by state but often by county and city within a state. Collecting the correct amount can mean applying the right combined rate for the buyer's specific location. This is part of why sellers with nexus in many places often turn to software or a professional, because getting the rate right at scale by hand is impractical.

## Keeping it in view

The practical habit is to keep a rough running picture of where your sales go and how much goes to each state, so you can see a threshold approaching before you cross it. You do not need perfect figures to stay aware; you need enough to know which states are getting close. That awareness is what tells you when it is time to register, start collecting, or call a professional.
